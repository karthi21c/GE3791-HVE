GE3791 Human Values and Ethics

How democratic, secular and scientific values guide the way we live, work and decide, and how to put them into practice. Two credits, 30 syllabus periods, with one extra practice period in every unit.

Course overview

This course introduces the values and ethical principles that the Constitution of India places at the centre of public life: democratic, secular and scientific. You study them, understand them and apply them in daily life and in your future profession.

What the course aims to do

  • Create awareness of the values and ethics in the Constitution of India.
  • Sensitise you to the democratic values a modern society must uphold.
  • Build respect for all people, whatever their religion or other affiliations.
  • Develop scientific temper and critical thinking.
  • Promote a sense of responsibility and an understanding of a citizen's duties.

The five units

Unit 1: Democratic Values

Equality, liberty, fraternity, freedom, justice, pluralism, tolerance, respect for all, freedom of expression and citizen participation in governance. World democracies: the French Revolution, American Independence and the Indian freedom movement.

Reading text: John Stuart Mill, On Liberty (excerpts). Periods: 01 Jul to 15 Jul, 7 periods (6 from the syllabus and 1 practice period).

Unit 2: Secular Values

Secular values and how secularism is interpreted in India: keeping the state apart from religion, accepting all faiths and encouraging non-discriminatory practices.

Reading text: Ram Puniyani, Secularism in India: Concept and Practice (excerpt). Periods: 17 Jul to 31 Jul, 7 periods (6 from the syllabus and 1 practice period).

Unit 3: Scientific Values

Inductive and deductive thinking, proposing and testing a hypothesis, validating facts with evidence, skepticism and empiricism, rationalism and scientific temper.

Reading text: Antony Michaelis R, The Scientific Temper (excerpt). Periods: 03 Aug to 24 Aug, 7 periods (6 from the syllabus and 1 practice period).

Unit 4: Social Ethics

Using ethical reasoning on social problems: gender bias and gender violence, social discrimination, constitutional protection and policies, inclusive practices.

Reading text: Yuval Noah Harari, 21 Lessons for the 21st Century (excerpt). Periods: 26 Aug to 09 Sep, 7 periods (6 from the syllabus and 1 practice period).

Unit 5: Scientific Ethics

Transparency and fairness in science, inventions that help society, unfair use of inventions, and the role and responsibility of scientists today.

Reading text: Kai Bird and Martin J. Sherwin, American Prometheus: The Triumph and Tragedy of J. Robert Oppenheimer (excerpt). Periods: 11 Sep to 05 Oct, 7 periods (6 from the syllabus and 1 practice period).

How we teach it

Every unit has six syllabus periods and one extra practice period. The practice period carries no marks. We recap the unit, explain ideas to a partner, practise short written answers and clear doubts. You can answer first in Tamil and then say or write it in English. AI tools help with simplified summaries, practice quizzes and feedback, and you always check what they give you against the syllabus text.

Study support

Before class

Read the simplified summary for the next topic. Write down two words or ideas you did not understand.

In practice periods

Use the answer frame: define the idea, give one example, link it to a course outcome, then conclude in one line.

Using AI well

Ask an AI tool to explain a topic simply or quiz you. Check every answer against your notes and the reading text. Do not type personal details into any AI tool.
